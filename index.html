The story

One stormy night.
One very literal robot.

Down at the Licton Light electric station, Watt can only follow instructions he can read, word for word, and the storm has blown the pages of his Start-Up Book all over the neighborhood. The pages that are left came out scrambled, and so did Watt's sentences.

  • Eleven chapters set in a real Seattle neighborhood: the park and its red spring, the school, the pond (seen from the sidewalk), a coffee shop, a college library and the police station.
  • A cast to love: word-collecting Wren, steady Milo, story-telling Grandpa Gil, a bossy duck named Duchess, and Watt, who hears "it's raining cats and dogs" and looks for the cats.
  • Fix a page and a street of houses lights up on the station's map, until the whole neighborhood glows for the Lantern Walk.

The reading

Words are the key to every door.

There are no worksheets here. The language is the broken school marquee, the soggy page on the back fence, the muddled coffee menu and the clues on a police case board, so every question has a reason.

  • Covers K–5 reading and language: letter sounds, phonics and spelling, vocabulary, grammar, punctuation, reading stories and nonfiction, and writing better sentences.
  • Getting ready for MAP Growth? An optional mode follows the skills on your child's grade's MAP Growth Reading and Language Usage plans.
  • Answers are taps and drags: pick a word, slide tiles into order, build a word from letter tiles, or tap the mistake. No keyboard needed.
  • 40 original reading passages, from short signs and menus to stories, poems and nonfiction, each with its own questions.

Reading, not listening

Voiced where it helps.
Quiet where it counts.

Every story line is spoken, so early readers can follow along on their own. But when a question is testing reading, the voice reads only the instructions, never the words being tested, so your child does the reading.

  • Kindergarten and 1st grade players get simpler story wording and more listening and picture questions.
  • A grown-up can turn on "read everything to me", and the parent area keeps reading and listening answers apart so you can see both.

Teach me how

Stuck? Mr. Alder will show you.

Every question has a Teach me how button. It opens Wren's classroom, where her teacher walks through the idea at the board with fresh examples and asks the class along the way.

  • A short, spoken lesson for every skill in the game.
  • It teaches the idea, then sends you back to try, without giving away the answer.

Explore and collect

Books, critters and a case file.

Walk the neighborhood by lantern light. The park's boardwalk leads to a spring with red, iron-rich mud, and every place has something to find.

  • 15 little books hidden in Little Free Libraries to read alone or have read aloud, including "Wires Down? Stay Back!"
  • Night critters in every scene, from owls to banana slugs, each with a real nature fact.
  • Wren's word notebook, a case file of clues, and a sticker for every skill your child masters.

After the storm

Games, a treehouse and daily work orders.

The adventure ends, but Licton Springs keeps going. Sparks earned along the way buy raincoat colors, lantern styles and hats for Watt.

  • Seven word games in the arcade, like Duck Rhymes, Comma Catcher and Synonym Switchboard.
  • Watt's daily work orders bring a few fresh questions each day.
  • A treehouse to fill with the things you collect.

For grown-ups

Licton Springs Language was made by a parent in Seattle for their own young reader, as the sister game to Maple Leaf Math. Here's what you can expect.

📈 A real picture of progress

The parent area shows every reading and language strand by level, recent answers, time played, the reading-versus-listening split and the skills that need more work.

🎯 Practice the skills on MAP Growth

Turn on MAP Growth mode and questions follow the skills on your child's grade's MAP Growth Reading and Language Usage plans, from well below to well above grade level. Enter their latest scores as starting points, and the parent area tracks an estimated level on the RIT scale.

🧭 Set it to your child

Pick a grade from K to 5 at first launch, or stick to grade-level skills without MAP Growth. You can also weight the areas you want to practice more.

⚡ Safety, modeled right

The kids never go near wires, poles or the transformer yard. Grandpa Gil and the lineworkers handle anything electrical, and the story says why.

⏳ No pressure

No timers on the questions, no streaks to lose, no loot boxes. Set a daily time limit if you like, and the story always ends at a natural stopping point.

🔒 Private by design

No ads, accounts, analytics or tracking, and the app never goes online. Move progress between devices with a save file you export yourself. Privacy policy

🧩 Accessible

A dyslexia-friendly font, extra line spacing, adjustable text size, captions, big buttons, color never carrying meaning alone, and reduced motion respected.

🛡️ Grown-ups only, behind a gate

The parent area and the unlock sit behind a grown-ups check. Your child only ever sees "ask a grown-up".
