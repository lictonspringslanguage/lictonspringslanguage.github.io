Support

Found a bug, spotted a typo or a question with the wrong answer, or have an idea? We'd love to hear from you.

Get in touch

Email bugjugapp@gmail.com. It helps to tell us your device, the chapter or place in the story, and what you expected to happen. If you use GitHub, you can also open an issue.

Common questions

What ages is it for?

Kids from kindergarten to 5th grade, roughly ages 5 to 11. The story is written for about ages 6 to 9 and every story line is spoken, so early readers can play on their own. The questions adapt to the player, so older or younger kids get reading and language work at their own level.

How do I get to the parent area?

On the title screen, tap the gear (⚙️) in the bottom corner. (From a scene, open the menu ☰ and tap Title screen first.) A grown-ups check asks you to press and hold a button for 3 seconds, then answer a quick question.

How do I change the grade?

In the parent area, open the Settings tab and pick a grade from K to 5. You can also choose whether to follow the NWEA MAP Growth Reading and Language Usage plans for that grade, or only grade-level skills. The game adjusts from the next question on.

Does it help with the MAP Growth test?

It practices the skills MAP Growth Reading and Language Usage measure. With MAP Growth turned on, questions follow the skills on your child's grade's plans, which reach well below and above grade level. You can enter your child's latest scores in the parent area as starting levels, and it shows the game's estimate of their level on the RIT scale. Every passage and question is our own, not a real test question, and the estimate isn't an official score.

Why doesn't the voice read the questions?

The story is fully voiced, but when a question is testing reading, the voice reads only the instructions so your child does the reading. Kindergarten and 1st grade players get more listening questions. If your child needs everything read aloud, turn on Read everything to me in the parent area; those answers are counted as listening, and the parent area shows reading and listening separately.

Is there a dyslexia-friendly option?

Yes. The parent area's Settings tab has a dyslexia-friendly font (OpenDyslexic), extra line spacing and a text size setting.

How do I move progress to a new phone or tablet?

In the parent area's Settings tab, tap Export save file and send it to the new device (AirDrop, Files or email work well). On the new device, install Licton Springs Language, open the parent area and tap Import save file. Each device keeps its own save until you do this.

I bought the full story. How do I get it on another device?

When the free chapters end, Wren asks for a grown-up. Tap I'm a grown-up, pass the check and tap Restore purchase. Signed in with the same Apple ID or Google account (or in the same Family Sharing group on Apple devices), the unlock comes back at no charge.

The purchase says it's waiting for approval.

That's Ask to Buy: the family organizer gets a request on their device. Once they approve it, the story unlocks.

My child is stuck on a puzzle.

Wrong answers bring a hint, then a stronger hint, then a walkthrough and a similar question, so no one stays stuck for long. The Teach me how button on every question opens a short lesson with Mr. Alder. You can always walk back to places you've already visited, too.

Does it need the internet?

No. Licton Springs Language works fully offline, even in airplane mode.

Is the free part really free?

Yes. The first four chapters (Lights Out through The Electric Station) and the treehouse are free with no time limit. One $4.99 purchase unlocks the rest of the story, the arcade and Watt's work orders. There are no ads, no subscriptions and nothing else for sale.
